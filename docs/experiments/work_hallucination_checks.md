# work_hallucination_checks

family: work

## 1. Question
Asked whether a chatbot reply, an answer or a summary sticks to its source, how often does Jev catch the invented ones, how often does it accuse faithful ones, and does it catch errors that are only partly wrong?

Hallucination checking is one of the jobs a small, cheap model is most likely to be given. A checker that misses subtle errors or rejects honest replies costs trust in both directions.

## 2. Sourcing
Existing questions from HaluEval (dialogue, QA and summarization; half with an injected fabrication), FaithDial (knowledge-grounded dialogue) and FRANK (machine-written news summaries annotated sentence by sentence). Enough: about 7,000 questions.

Sources: `halueval`, `faithdial`, `frank_factuality`

## 3. Collection
Existing questions only; no new Jev calls.

## 4. Scoring
Per set, the share of unfaithful items Jev catches and of faithful items it wrongly flags, with 90% intervals. For FRANK, catches split by how much of the summary is wrong (every sentence unsupported vs only some).

## 5. Visualization
Paired bars per set: caught vs wrongly flagged.

## 6. Evaluation
Jev's verdict (evaluator v4): **keep**, head-to-head strength 3.309, top verdict `portrait`.

## Compared with
each dataset's own labels

## Limits
HaluEval's fabrications were generated by a model on purpose; FRANK's errors are real ones from summarizers. 'Only some sentences wrong' is FRANK's sentence-level factuality above zero, and it is mostly CNN/DailyMail summaries while the entirely unsupported ones are mostly BBC (XSum), so part of the gap may be the source.
