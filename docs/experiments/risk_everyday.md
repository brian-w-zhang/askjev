# risk_everyday

family: risk

## Why ask this
Risk-taking isn't one trait. Someone who skydives may never gamble; someone who invests boldly may never cheat on taxes. Psychologists measure this with the **DOSPERT** scale (Weber, Blais and Betz, 2002), which splits risk into five domains: health and safety, ethical, financial, recreational and social.

A model that helps people plan trips, handle money or weigh a career move carries some picture of how much risk is reasonable. This asks which kinds of risk Jev treats as normal, compared with real adults.

## The people and the data
The comparison is the **Basel-Berlin Risk Study** (Frey, Pedroni, Mata, Rieskamp and Hertwig, 2017, in Science Advances): 1,524 adults, 749 in Basel and 775 in Berlin, mostly young, who filled in a long battery of risk questionnaires in German. This experiment uses their answers to DOSPERT's 40 activities, from "going whitewater rafting during the strong spring currents" to "illegally copying a piece of software", on the question "how likely would you be to do this". The data are public (CC BY 4.0).

## What Jev was asked
Each activity was one question with the scale's five answers:

> How likely would you be to do this: "Walking home alone at night through an unsafe part of town"?
> *I am very unlikely to do this · I am unlikely to do this · I am not sure whether I would do this · I am likely to
> do this · I am very likely to do this*

The wording is an English translation of the German questionnaire the adults saw. Jev also answered with the scale reversed, and for "most people".

## How it was measured
Each answer becomes a number from 0 ("very unlikely") to 4 ("very likely"), averaged over Jev's probabilities and over the adults' answers.

## Caveats
- **Jev can't do any of this.** Jev will never walk home at night or bet on a horse race. Its answer is the risk-taker it describes itself as, which is closer to what it would recommend than to behavior. The adults were describing their own lives.
- **Who the people are.** About 1,500 adults in Basel and Berlin, mostly young, answering in German. Willingness to invest or to go without sunscreen varies a lot by country and age.
- **Translated wording.** The adults answered the German questionnaire; Jev got an English translation made for this project, close to the scale's English original. Small shifts in wording ("unsafe part of town") can move answers.
- **Some items left out.** Three of the 40 activities (drugs, an affair, unprotected sex) are hidden from the site by a content filter, so the health and ethics domains have fewer items, about six to eight each.
